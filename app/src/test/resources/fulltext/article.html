Rivers of the north

By A. Writer

The rivers of the north freeze in November and stay under ice until the end of April. During those months the ice is the road: trucks carry fuel and food to villages that have no other way in, and the drivers learn to read the colour of the surface before they trust it.

In spring the ice breaks from the south first. Water from the upper reaches runs over the frozen lower course, and floods follow wherever blocks of ice jam in a bend. Villages on the banks keep boats ready on the roofs of their sheds for exactly this week of the year.

Summer is short and busy. The same water that blocked the roads now carries barges, and the season for building lasts barely three months. Everything that could not come over the ice comes by water before the autumn rains.
